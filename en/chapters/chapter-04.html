A Little Boy's Lunch Bag

People loved listening to my Master tell stories. They always wanted Him to say more. But happy moments always seem to pass the fastest. Before we knew it, the setting sun had painted the horizon orange-red, and the evening sky had begun to glow.

My Master asked the disciples beside Him what they should give the crowd to eat. Some of them answered that even if they sent everyone home to find food, it would take a long time for the entire crowd to disperse. The disciples began asking whether anyone among the people had brought food. Very few had.

Then a little boy stepped forward, carrying his own small lunch bag. His mother had prepared it for him. Inside were five small loaves of bread and two fish.

I almost wanted to swoop down and stop him, because he clearly had no idea how many people were there. From high above, I could see them covering the hills and filling the valley.

“How could so little food possibly feed so many people?”

Yet the boy's simple offering seemed to please my Master greatly. My Master took his lunch bag. He gave thanks, then broke the loaves and divided the fish, handing them to His disciples. The disciples began distributing the food among the people.

I expected them to run out almost immediately. But the food kept moving. One portion after another. One pair of hands after another. From the hands of the disciples into the hands of the crowd.

I circled above the hillside, watching. Some people stared in amazement at the food in their hands. Others simply lowered their heads and ate quietly. The fragrance of warm bread and fish spread across the hillside.

Before long, everyone had eaten their fill. The children got up and gathered together to play. The adults sat in small groups, watching the last light of the setting sun fade in the west.

Still, I did not understand. There had been only five loaves and two fish. How could there possibly have been enough for so many?

My Master did not hurry away. Instead, He told the disciples to gather the remaining pieces so that nothing would be wasted.

The disciples moved through the crowd, gathering what was left, one piece at a time.

I watched from above. One basket. Then another. And another. Until, at last, they had filled twelve baskets.

My eyes widened. Five loaves. Two fish. Such a small amount of food had fed so many people—and twelve baskets were still left over.

My Master often prayed alone in the early morning. Sometimes in the forest. Sometimes on a mountain. Only after He had finished praying would He go with His disciples into one village after another.

He seemed very happy living among human beings. He told them stories. He healed them. He stayed with them. But He was also often sorrowful because of them.

I heard Him praying for those people. Sometimes I could not understand why He prayed. From where I watched, it seemed that nothing changed immediately after He prayed. The people still argued. The sick still needed healing. Those who opposed Him still came looking for Him.

Yet, little by little, I began to notice something strange. After my Master prayed, He Himself seemed different. He became quieter. More steadfast. More courageous. He was able to face each new day. Even when people accused Him harshly, He did not turn away.

And I began to understand one thing: Everything my Master did seemed to begin with those quiet moments of prayer. And so I began to wonder:

What would human beings do to my Master next?
